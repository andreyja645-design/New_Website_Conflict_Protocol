## The scene

This can happen anywhere. A kitchen. A meeting. A text thread. No audience required.

**You:** "Please do not comment on my body."

**Them:** "You are too sensitive."

There it is. The trapdoor. The conversation drops from what they did into whether your reaction is the correct size.

If you step into that room, you may spend the next ten minutes proving you are not dramatic, fragile, humorless, angry, intense, needy, or whatever label got tossed onto the floor.

## What's actually happening

This is [minimizing](/patterns/minimizing/) and sometimes [gaslighting](/patterns/gaslighting/). The behavior disappears. Your reaction becomes the evidence. If you are "too sensitive," then they do not have to address the comment, the broken agreement, the boundary, or the impact.

This is different from the public joke page. That one is about the social stage: the laugh, the witnesses, the group pressure. This one is the deflection mechanic itself: "your reaction is the problem."

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor">
  <p>"My sensitivity is not the issue. The comment is the issue."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Tiny. Sharp. Hard to wander away from.

## The script

**You:** "Please do not comment on my body."

**Them:** "You are too sensitive."

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"My sensitivity is not the issue. The comment is the issue."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

You refuse the new topic.

**Them:** "I cannot say anything around you."

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"You can say plenty. I am asking you not to make comments about my body."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The fake extreme gets returned to the actual boundary.

**Them:** "You always take things the wrong way."

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"I am not debating my whole personality. I am asking for this comment to stop."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

No lifetime performance review. One behavior.

**Them:** "Calm down. Your tone is the problem."

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"I can say it more calmly. The point still stands."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

This is how you repair delivery without donating the issue.

**Them:** "It was harmless."

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"You do not have to agree with my reaction. Do not make that comment again."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The boundary does not need their agreement to exist. Rude of reality, but helpful.

## Variations

If they say you are dramatic:

<ScriptBlock label="Dramatic">
  <p>"I am not debating whether I am dramatic. I am naming what I want to stop."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If they say you misunderstood:

<ScriptBlock label="Misunderstood">
  <p>"Maybe you meant it differently. I am telling you how I want this handled going forward."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If tone is genuinely worth repairing:

<ScriptBlock label="Repair plus return">
  <p>"I could have said that better. The original point still matters."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If they keep rating your reaction:

<ScriptBlock label="Repeat">
  <p>"The issue is the behavior, not the size of my reaction."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If they mock the boundary:

<ScriptBlock label="Exit">
  <p>"This is no longer useful. I am stopping here."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If you need the smallest possible version:

<ScriptBlock label="Smallest">
  <p>"The comment is the issue."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The win is not proving you are the perfect amount of sensitive. That job has no salary and terrible hours. The win is keeping the behavior in the frame.
