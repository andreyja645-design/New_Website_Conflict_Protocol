## The scene

It starts with the classic harmless sentence. The one that arrives at 5:55, wearing tiny shoes and carrying a suitcase.

**Boss:** "Before you log off, can you just clean up the client deck?"

**You:** "Sure, I can tidy it up."

**Boss:** "Great. Also make the numbers consistent, rewrite the intro, add the appendix, and be ready to walk the client through it tomorrow morning."

**You:** "Oh. Okay, I can try."

And now your small yes has become a full project with a fake mustache.

## What's actually happening

This is [scope creep](/patterns/scope-creep/) with a side of [moving goalposts](/patterns/moving-goalposts/). The ask expands, but the conversation keeps pretending nothing changed.

The trick is the word "just." It makes every extra piece sound too small to question. Then "ownership" arrives, which is workplace for "please feel morally weird about having limits."

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor">
  <p>"That is beyond the original ask. I can do the cleanup tonight, or we can reset the scope for tomorrow."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The job is not to prove you are helpful. You already were. The job is to stop the first yes from becoming an all-access pass.

## The script

**Boss:** "Can you just clean up the deck before you leave?"

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"I can clean up formatting before I leave."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Name the exact yes. Tiny fence, huge savings.

**Boss:** "Perfect. Also rewrite the opening and add the appendix."

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"That adds scope. I can do formatting tonight, or the rewrite tomorrow. Which one matters more?"</p>
</ScriptBlock>

This turns "be flexible" into a choice. Choices are annoying to pressure. Excellent.

**Boss:** "It should not take that long."

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"If it is small, it can wait until tomorrow. If it is urgent, something else needs to move."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

You are not debating the time estimate. You are asking for the tradeoff.

**Boss:** "I thought you wanted more ownership."

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"I do. Ownership includes being clear about scope and priority."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Lovely little reframe. You did not accept the character test.

**Boss:** "Fine. Then just make it client-ready."

<ScriptBlock label="You">
  <p>"I am available for the original cleanup tonight. Client-ready is a new request, so I need a new timeline."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Broken record, but not robotic. Same line, fresh jacket.

## Variations

If they keep adding "one more thing":

<ScriptBlock label="Scope reset">
  <p>"Before I add anything else, we need to reset scope, deadline, and priority."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If they say everyone else is pitching in:

<ScriptBlock label="No team-player trial">
  <p>"I am not debating commitment. I am clarifying what work fits in the time available."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If they need an answer right now:

<ScriptBlock label="Rushed answer">
  <p>"If you need an answer this second, I can only commit to the original cleanup."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If they ask in Slack after hours:

<ScriptBlock label="Written version">
  <p>"To confirm: I agreed to formatting. The rewrite and appendix are additional scope. Please confirm what should move if those are due tomorrow morning."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If you already said yes too fast:

<ScriptBlock label="Reset">
  <p>"I answered too quickly. Looking at the full scope, I need to reset what I can realistically complete."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If they punish the limit:

<ScriptBlock label="Documentable">
  <p>"Please put the revised expectation in writing so I can respond to the actual scope."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The clean move is boring on purpose: original ask, new scope, priority tradeoff. Do not let "just" borrow your whole evening.
