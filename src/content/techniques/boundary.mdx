## What it is in one paragraph

A boundary is not a wish for the other person to behave. It is your line about what you will do next. It gets stronger when the sentence is simple and the action behind it is real.

## When it works / when it backfires

Use it when the conversation needs a clear no, limit, or condition for continuing.

Do not use it as a threat you cannot or will not follow. A boundary with no action behind it becomes decoration.

</ScriptBlock>

  <p>"I will continue when we can speak respectfully."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Common mistakes

- Calling a request a boundary: "You need to stop."
- Adding a long defense after the line.
- Setting a consequence you will not follow.
- Making the first version too dramatic.
- Apologizing until the boundary collapses.

The clean version is often the strongest version.

## Patterns this counters

Boundary helps with scope creep, loyalty tests, empathy traps, and minimizing because each one tries to make your line seem unreasonable.

<ScriptBlock label="Scope creep version">
  <p>"I am available for the original request only."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The point is not to control them. The point is to be clear about you.
