## What it is in one paragraph

Reframing changes the center of the conversation. Instead of accepting their frame, you name a more useful one: the actual request, the next step, the original agreement, or what you can control.

## When it works / when it backfires

Use it when the conversation has drifted into comparison, accusation, side characters, or old history.

Do not use it to dodge a direct question you really should answer. If the heat is already high, pair it with a boundary.

## Common mistakes

- Reframing without answering the actual issue.
- Making the reframe sound like a lecture.
- Moving too fast past someone's real concern.
- Using "the real issue" as a way to dominate.
- Forgetting to land on a concrete next step.

Good reframes are short. They point, then stop pointing.

## Patterns this counters

Reframe helps with triangulation, comparison, DARVO, and moving goalposts because those patterns win by dragging you onto the wrong field.

<ScriptBlock label="Triangulation version">
  <p>"Other people's choices are not the question. The question is what I can do."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The point is not verbal gymnastics. The point is choosing the ground before you answer.
