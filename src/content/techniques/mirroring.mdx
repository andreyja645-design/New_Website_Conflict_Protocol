## What it is in one paragraph

Mirroring repeats the shape of what you heard, calmly and without adding a counterattack. It slows the conversation down and gives the other person a chance to clarify, soften, or own the claim.

## When it works / when it backfires

Use it when the pressure is vague, implied, or slippery.

Do not use it in front of an audience if repeating the attack would deepen the embarrassment. In public, keep it shorter or move offline.

## Copy-paste scripts

<ScriptBlock label="Let down">
  <p>"So you are saying you feel let down."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Fault">
  <p>"It sounds like you mean I was not there for you."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Follow">
  <p>"Let me make sure I follow you."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Wanted more">
  <p>"What I am hearing is that you wanted more from me."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Blame">
  <p>"If I have got this right, you are saying it is my fault."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Clarify">
  <p>"Are you asking me to change my answer, or naming that you are upset?"</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Common mistakes

- Mirroring with a mocking tone.
- Turning the mirror into a paragraph.
- Repeating cruel words in a public setting.
- Using it when you already know the answer and need a boundary.
- Asking for clarity, then arguing with every clarification.

The mirror should be clean enough that the pressure has nowhere cute to hide.

## Patterns this counters

Mirroring helps with triangulation, DARVO, and comparison because it forces implied pressure into plain language.

<ScriptBlock label="Comparison version">
  <p>"So you are saying my answer should match what Maya chose."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The point is not to trap them. The point is to hear the actual ask before you answer.
