## What it is in one paragraph

Broken Record is steady repetition. You answer the same pressure with the same boundary, lightly rephrased. You are not trying to be clever. You are refusing to let each new angle create a new debate.

## When it works / when it backfires

Use it when the other person keeps repeating the same demand, guilt line, comparison, or loophole hunt.

Do not use it when the facts actually changed or when you owe a real answer. Broken Record is for pressure, not for dodging accountability.

## Copy-paste scripts

<ScriptBlock label="No topic">
  <p>"I will not be discussing that."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Same answer">
  <p>"My answer remains the same."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Already answered">
  <p>"I have already said what I have to say."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Position">
  <p>"I am not going to change my position on this."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Final">
  <p>"That is my final answer."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Softer repeat">
  <p>"I know this is frustrating. I am still not available."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Common mistakes

- Repeating with rising anger until it becomes a fight.
- Adding new explanations every time.
- Saying "final answer" and then reopening the negotiation.
- Using it before you know what your boundary is.
- Confusing consistency with coldness.

The power is in the lack of new material.

## Patterns this counters

Broken Record is strong against guilt trips, loyalty tests, scope creep, and silent treatment follow-ups because each one often returns wearing slightly different wording.

<ScriptBlock label="Scope creep version">
  <p>"I can do the summary, not the full report."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The point is to let repetition work for you for once.
