## What it is in one paragraph

Tactic Naming makes the move visible: the subject changed, the standard shifted, guilt entered the room, the crowd got invited. The goal is not to diagnose the person. It is to stop the tactic from working invisibly.

## When it works / when it backfires

Use it when the pattern is clear and naming it will help you return to the point.

Do not use it as a mic drop. If you sound like you are labeling the person instead of the move, the conversation may become a fight about the label.

## Common mistakes

- Naming the person instead of the move.
- Using the biggest possible label first.
- Saying it with courtroom energy.
- Naming the tactic and forgetting your boundary.
- Expecting the other person to thank you for the clarity.

The best version is almost boring.

## Patterns this counters

Tactic Naming helps with DARVO, moving goalposts, triangulation, and guilt trips because those moves depend on staying unnamed.

<ScriptBlock label="DARVO version">
  <p>"This is shifting from what happened to whether I am bad for bringing it up."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The point is not to sound clever. The point is to see the move before you answer it.
