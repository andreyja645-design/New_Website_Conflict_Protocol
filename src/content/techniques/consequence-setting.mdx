## What it is in one paragraph

Consequence Setting says what you will do if the pattern continues. It is not punishment. It is the action attached to your boundary: leave, pause, stop replying, document, reschedule, or escalate through the right channel.

## When it works / when it backfires

Use it after you have named the line and the line keeps getting crossed.

Do not use it as a bluff. Consequences lose strength when they are dramatic, vague, or impossible for you to follow.

## Copy-paste scripts

<ScriptBlock label="End call">
  <p>"If you keep speaking to me like that, I will end the conversation."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Step away">
  <p>"If this keeps happening, I am going to step away for now."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Take a break">
  <p>"If we cannot keep this respectful, I will need to take a break from it."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Leave">
  <p>"I will stay and talk, but if the insults continue, I am leaving."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Stop responding">
  <p>"If you keep bringing this up, I will not keep responding to it."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Work version">
  <p>"If my contribution is removed again, I will document the history and loop in the project lead."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Common mistakes

- Setting a consequence you will not carry out.
- Making the consequence bigger than the situation.
- Using it before a plain boundary would work.
- Explaining the consequence until it becomes a negotiation.
- Treating consequence as revenge.

Keep it small, real, and followable.

## Patterns this counters

Consequence Setting helps with scope creep, silent treatment, credit theft, and public shaming when the pattern has already ignored softer lines.

<ScriptBlock label="Silent treatment version">
  <p>"If I do not hear back by noon, I will make the plan without more input."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The point is not to control their next move. The point is to know yours.
