## What it is in one paragraph

Grey Rock is low-detail mode. You give short, plain replies so the other person has less to grab, twist, or turn into a fresh argument. It is not revenge silence. It is not a personality transplant. It is a narrow tool for high-friction conversations where extra emotion becomes extra ammunition.

## When it works / when it backfires

Use it when the conversation is bait, gossip, extraction, character debate, or the same demand wearing a new hat.

Do not use it when someone is asking a fair question, when you owe repair, when a child needs warmth, or when low response could make an unsafe person escalate. Grey Rock is for ordinary pressure, not for solving danger with a cute sentence.

## Common mistakes

- Announcing the technique. "I am grey-rocking you" is not Grey Rock. It is a new argument with a name tag.
- Using sarcasm. Dry is fine. Contempt gives the bait a handle.
- Giving one short line and then donating the paragraph anyway.
- Using Grey Rock on safe people who are genuinely trying.
- Staying in a bad situation because you are trying to be low-reaction.

The tone is boring receipt. A receipt does not defend itself. It does not explain why it is a receipt. It just records the transaction and sits there being magnificently uninteresting.

## Patterns this counters

Grey Rock is useful against gaslighting when the denial is not a good-faith memory mismatch. It helps you stop supplying details for the memory trial.

<ScriptBlock label="Gaslighting version">
  <p>"We remember it differently. I am not debating that further."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The point is not to sound clever. The point is to leave them with less to use.
