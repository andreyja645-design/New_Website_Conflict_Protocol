## What it is in one paragraph

Fogging is the calm art of agreeing with the tiny true part and refusing to fight the whole insult. You do not argue every exaggeration. You do not confess to the demand. You give the attack less wall to bounce off.

## When it works / when it backfires

Use it when someone is trying to pull you into a character debate, especially when the criticism has a speck of truth wrapped in a bucket of pressure.

Do not use it when clarity is needed, when the accusation is dangerous, or when agreeing with even a fragment would be used as a confession. Fogging is a pressure valve, not a legal statement.

## Copy-paste scripts

<ScriptBlock label="Tiny truth">
  <p>"You might be right about that."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Looks that way">
  <p>"I can see why it looks that way."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Fair point">
  <p>"That is a fair point."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Some truth">
  <p>"There is probably some truth to that."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Heard">
  <p>"I hear you."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Hold the line">
  <p>"You may be right about that part. My answer is still no."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Common mistakes

- Agreeing with the whole accusation.
- Adding a long defense after the fogging line.
- Using it with a smug tone.
- Fogging when you need a clean boundary.
- Treating "I hear you" as a promise to comply.

The tone is plain receipt, not surrender.

## Patterns this counters

Fogging is useful against DARVO, minimizing, and public shaming because it denies the attack a big emotional surface.

<ScriptBlock label="DARVO version">
  <p>"You may be right that my tone was tense. The issue is still what happened."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The point is to stop wrestling the whole accusation and keep your footing.
