## What it is in one paragraph

An I-Statement names what is happening for you and what you need next. It works best when the other person might still be reachable, or when you want the record to be clear without escalating into blame.

## When it works / when it backfires

Use it when you need to correct, object, or ask for a change while keeping the sentence clean.

Do not use it as a velvet wrapper for a boundary that needs to be direct. If someone keeps ignoring the I-Statement, graduate to a firmer tool.

## Copy-paste scripts

<ScriptBlock label="Tone">
  <p>"I feel uncomfortable when conversations go this way."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Respect">
  <p>"I am not okay with being spoken to like this."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Impact">

</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Pause">

</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Overload">
  <p>"I feel overwhelmed right now."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Credit">
  <p>"I am not comfortable with my draft being shared without my name attached."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Common mistakes

- Turning it into "I feel that you are terrible."
- Using it five times when one boundary would do.
- Explaining so much that the ask disappears.
- Asking for permission to have the feeling.
- Expecting one clean sentence to fix someone committed to ignoring you.

Keep it simple: impact, need, next step.

## Patterns this counters

I-Statements help with minimizing, credit theft, and public shaming because they make the impact hard to blur.

<ScriptBlock label="Minimizing version">
  <p>"I am not okay with that joke being made about me."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The point is not to win sympathy. The point is to put your position on the table.
