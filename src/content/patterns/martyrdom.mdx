## What it looks like

Martyrdom arrives wearing exhaustion as a crown. The message is not only "I am tired." It is "Because I suffered, you now owe me the answer I want."

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"I gave up so much for this family."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Do not worry about me. I will just suffer quietly."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"I suppose my sacrifices do not matter to you."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Suddenly the current request is buried under a mountain of history. You are not answering Tuesday. You are cross-examining twenty years.

## Why it works on you

Martyrdom hooks your fairness reflex. You may genuinely care about what they carried. You may also fear sounding cold if you separate their past effort from the current ask.

The pattern is not naming sacrifice. Naming sacrifice can be honest. The pattern is making sacrifice into a bill that only your yes can pay.

## The tell

Watch for the sigh-and-ledger combo: heavy tone, old sacrifices, and a present request that somehow becomes non-negotiable.

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor line">
  <p>"I know you have carried a lot. I am still keeping this boundary."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When they make it about debt">
  <p>"Your sacrifice matters, and it does not decide this for me."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When they accuse you of dismissal">
  <p>"I am not dismissing what you gave up. I am saying no to this request."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The tell is when compassion is treated like a blank check.

## The trap

The trap is trying to settle the historical account before you answer the present.

<ScriptBlock label="The line that feeds it">
  <p>"I know you did a lot, but I also helped, and I have been trying, and you never notice when I..."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Now you are in the old record room, dust everywhere, no exit sign. Bring it back to the current decision.

<ScriptBlock label="Better">
  <p>"I can respect what you did and still make a different choice now."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Exit line">
  <p>"I am not going to weigh every sacrifice to decide this."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Counters

Validation names the real pain. Boundary names the present limit. Broken Record keeps the ledger from reopening every two sentences.

<ScriptBlock label="Soft and firm">
  <p>"I hear that you feel unappreciated. I am not changing my answer."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Repeat">
  <p>"That does not work for me."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Smallest usable version">
  <p>"I respect that. I am still not doing this."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The win is not denying their effort. The win is refusing to let effort become ownership.
