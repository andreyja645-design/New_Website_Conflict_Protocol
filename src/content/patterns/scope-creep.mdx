## What it looks like

Scope creep starts with something manageable. Then another thing arrives. Then another. Somehow your "sure, I can help for ten minutes" is wearing a project manager badge.

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Since you are already helping, can you also clean up the rest?"</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"It is basically the same task."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"One more small thing, and then we are done."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The trick is that each add-on sounds too tiny to object to. The pile is not tiny.

## Why it works on you

Scope creep hooks reasonableness. You already said yes, so a little more can feel petty to resist. You may notice the problem only after your capacity has been quietly spent.

The pattern is not a follow-up request. Follow-ups are allowed. The pattern is treating your first yes as permission to keep expanding the job.

## The tell

Watch for "also," "just," "while you are there," and "basically the same." Those words are not evil. They are also excellent hiding places.

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor line">
  <p>"That is beyond the original ask. I can discuss it as a separate request."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When you can do part">
  <p>"I can do the summary, not the full report."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When the pile grows">
  <p>"Before I add anything, we need to reset the scope and timing."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The tell is expansion without renegotiation.

## The trap

The trap is absorbing each add-on quietly.

<ScriptBlock label="The line that feeds it">
  <p>"Okay, I can probably do that too. Just send it over."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

That line may buy ten seconds of peace and sell your evening at a discount.

<ScriptBlock label="Better">
  <p>"I agreed to the summary. The extra analysis needs a separate timeline."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Exit line">
  <p>"If the scope keeps changing, I am going to stop here and revisit it tomorrow."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Counters

Boundary names the edge. Broken Record repeats the original agreement. Consequence Setting helps when the add-ons keep arriving after you have been clear.

<ScriptBlock label="Boundary">
  <p>"I am available for the original request only."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Repeat">
  <p>"I can do the summary, not the full report."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Smallest usable version">
  <p>"That is outside the scope."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The win is not becoming unhelpful. The win is keeping help from becoming open-ended ownership.
