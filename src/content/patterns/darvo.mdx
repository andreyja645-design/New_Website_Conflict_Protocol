## What it looks like

You name something they did. They deny it, attack you for saying it, then cast themselves as the injured party.

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"I cannot believe you are accusing me after everything I have done."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"That did not happen, and honestly your tone is abusive."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"You are the one hurting me right now."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The subject changes from their behavior to your cruelty for noticing it. Very efficient. Very slippery.

## Why it works on you

DARVO hooks your wish to be fair. Once they sound wounded, you may drop the original issue and start proving you are not mean, unfair, dramatic, or unsafe to talk to.

The pattern is not someone having feelings about feedback. The pattern is using those feelings to erase the behavior you named.

## The tell

The tell is the three-step turn: denial, attack, reversal. If the conversation becomes a trial about whether you are the real problem, pause.

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor line">
  <p>"We can talk about my tone after we address what happened."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When they claim victimhood">
  <p>"I am not debating who is the victim. I am naming the behavior."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When they make noticing the issue the issue">
  <p>"The issue is the comment you made, not whether I am unfair for noticing it."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

If the spotlight moved without your consent, move it back.

## The trap

The trap is accepting the new courtroom.

<ScriptBlock label="The line that feeds it">
  <p>"I am not attacking you. I am a good person, and I would never try to hurt you."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

That answer feels kind, but now the case is your intent. Their original behavior gets a quiet vacation.

<ScriptBlock label="Better">
  <p>"I am willing to discuss my tone later. Right now I am addressing what happened."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Exit line">
  <p>"If this stays focused on attacking me, I am going to pause the conversation."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Counters

Tactic Naming can expose the reversal. Reframe returns the focus to the behavior. Boundary ends the attack loop if the conversation cannot stay usable.

<ScriptBlock label="Name it lightly">
  <p>"This is shifting from what happened to whether I am bad for bringing it up."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Return to point">
  <p>"The next step is addressing the comment, not rating my character."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Smallest usable version">
  <p>"Back to what happened."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The win is not getting them to admit the pattern. The win is not abandoning the original issue.
