## What it looks like

Moving goalposts turn completion into a mirage. You meet the agreed standard, and the standard quietly grows legs.

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Yes, you finished that, but now I need the full deck too."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"That is a start, but I expected more initiative."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"I know I said one section, but if you really owned this..."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

You keep running. The finish line keeps learning new tricks.

## Why it works on you

This pattern hooks conscientiousness. If you care about doing good work, you may treat every new demand as evidence that you missed something instead of evidence that the agreement changed.

The pattern is not normal iteration. Normal iteration names the change. Moving goalposts pretend the new bar was obvious all along.

## The tell

Watch for vague upgrades after the fact: "more polished," "real ownership," "not just the minimum." Foggy standards are easy to move.

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor line">
  <p>"I completed the agreed scope. What is the new standard before I do more?"</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When the ask changes">
  <p>"That is a new request, so let's define it separately."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When they refuse to close the original">
  <p>"I can discuss the next version after we close the original ask."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The tell is retroactive expectation. If they cannot name the original agreement, neither can you satisfy it.

## The trap

The trap is apologizing for not meeting a standard that did not exist yet.

<ScriptBlock label="The line that feeds it">
  <p>"Sorry, I should have known you wanted the full deck too. I can stay late and fix it."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Maybe staying late is your choice. But do not confuse a new request with a failure.

<ScriptBlock label="Better">
  <p>"I can take on the additional deck if we reset timeline and priority."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Exit line">
  <p>"I am not adding work until the new scope is clear."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Counters

Broken Record protects the original agreement. Boundary stops surprise expansion. Tactic Naming makes the shift visible without turning it into a speech.

<ScriptBlock label="Repeat">
  <p>"The agreed deliverable is complete."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Name the shift">
  <p>"This is a different standard than the one we set."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Smallest usable version">
  <p>"That is a new ask."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The win is not refusing all change. The win is making change honest.
