## What it looks like

Minimizing does not answer the impact. It argues that the impact is too small to count.

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Relax, it was just a joke."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"You are making a huge deal out of nothing."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Nobody else would be upset by this."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The move is simple: if your reaction is too much, they do not have to look at the behavior.

## Why it works on you

Minimizing hooks self-doubt. If you already worry about being too much, you may start lowering your own discomfort until it fits their preferred story.

The pattern is not someone disagreeing about severity. The pattern is using severity as a door lock: if it was "only" a joke, you are not allowed to set a limit.

## The tell

Watch for tiny words doing heavy work: "just," "only," "nothing," "not a big deal." They shrink the event so your boundary looks oversized.

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor line">
  <p>"It may have been a joke to you. I do not want it repeated."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When they debate seriousness">
  <p>"I am not debating whether it was serious enough. It did not work for me."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When impact gets dismissed">
  <p>"The impact matters here, and I am asking you to stop."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The tell is being asked to prove your discomfort deserves admission.

## The trap

The trap is arguing for the size of your hurt.

<ScriptBlock label="The line that feeds it">
  <p>"It really was serious because you said it in front of everyone, and I felt embarrassed, and it reminded me of..."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

You can share context with safe people. With a minimizer, context often becomes more material to minimize.

<ScriptBlock label="Better">
  <p>"You do not have to agree with my reaction. I am asking you not to repeat it."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Exit line">
  <p>"If this stays about whether I am allowed to object, I am done for now."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Counters

I-Statement names the impact. Boundary names the limit. Broken Record keeps the debate from becoming a measurement contest.

<ScriptBlock label="I-statement">
  <p>"I am not okay with being joked about that way."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Repeat">
  <p>"Do not make that joke about me again."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Smallest usable version">
  <p>"Do not repeat it."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The win is not getting permission to care. The win is acting like your limit counts.
