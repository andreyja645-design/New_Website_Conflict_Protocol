## What it looks like

Triangulation brings a third person into a two-person decision. Sometimes the person is real. Sometimes they are suspiciously unavailable for comment.

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Everyone else understands this."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Alex already agreed, so I do not know why you are being difficult."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Your sister never makes this such a big thing."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The room gets crowded fast. Your answer starts competing with a committee you did not invite.

## Why it works on you

Triangulation hooks belonging and status. Nobody enjoys feeling like the lone unreasonable person. You may start arguing with the third party's supposed opinion instead of answering the actual request.

The pattern is not useful context. Useful context can be checked and discussed plainly. The pattern is using other people as pressure.

## The tell

Watch for the missing direct ask. The conversation gets full of what everyone else thinks before you are told exactly what they want from you.

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor line">
  <p>"I am not discussing Alex. What are you asking me for?"</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When they cite the crowd">
  <p>"Other people's opinions do not change my answer."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When they pull in family or coworkers">
  <p>"Let's keep this between us and the decision in front of us."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The tell is social proof used as a shortcut around your consent.

## The trap

The trap is litigating the third party.

<ScriptBlock label="The line that feeds it">
  <p>"Alex only agreed because you pressured them, and my sister does not even know the whole story."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Maybe true. Also a detour. The present decision still needs your answer.

<ScriptBlock label="Better">
  <p>"Their choice is theirs. My answer is about what I can do."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Exit line">
  <p>"I am not continuing if this becomes a comparison panel."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Counters

Reframe pulls the talk back to the actual request. Boundary keeps other people's choices from becoming your obligation. Tactic Naming helps when the crowd keeps reappearing.

<ScriptBlock label="Reframe">
  <p>"The question is whether I am available, and I am not."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Name it">
  <p>"Bringing other people in is adding pressure, not clarity."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Smallest usable version">
  <p>"That is between you and them."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The win is not proving the crowd wrong. The win is making your decision without an imaginary scoreboard.
