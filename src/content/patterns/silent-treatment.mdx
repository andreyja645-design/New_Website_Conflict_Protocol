## What it looks like

Silent treatment is not someone taking space to cool down. It is silence used as control: no clarity, no timeline, just punishment with a blank face.

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Fine. If that is how you feel."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Do whatever you want."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"You can figure out what you did wrong."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Then the messages stop, the room freezes, and you are expected to do emotional detective work.

## Why it works on you

Silent treatment hooks your need for resolution. If open loops make you anxious, you may start apologizing, explaining, or offering concessions just to get contact back.

The pattern is not a pause. A healthy pause has a return point. Punitive silence makes you pay to reopen the door.

## The tell

Watch for silence with no boundary attached. "I need an hour and will text at seven" is space. "Guess what you did" is a trap.

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor line">
  <p>"I am available to talk when you are ready to speak directly."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When you feel pulled to chase">
  <p>"I will not chase silence. We can revisit this later."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="When you need to step back">
  <p>"I am stepping back now and keeping my boundary."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The tell is your anxiety becoming the only engine in the conversation.

## The trap

The trap is paying the silence tax.

<ScriptBlock label="The line that feeds it">
  <p>"Please just tell me what I did. I am sorry. I will do whatever you want if you talk to me."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

That line may reopen contact, but it teaches the silence that it works.

<ScriptBlock label="Better">
  <p>"I am open to a direct conversation. I am not guessing or bargaining."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Exit line">
  <p>"If you choose not to respond, I will make my plans without more input."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Counters

Boundary keeps you from chasing. Broken Record repeats the only available door: direct conversation. Consequence Setting helps when silence is used to block practical decisions.

<ScriptBlock label="Boundary">
  <p>"I will talk when you are ready to use words."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Repeat">
  <p>"I am available for a direct conversation."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Smallest usable version">
  <p>"I am not guessing."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The win is not forcing them to talk. The win is not letting silence run your choices.
