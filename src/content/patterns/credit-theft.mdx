## What it looks like

Credit theft takes your draft, idea, labor, or strategy and presents it as someone else's work. Sometimes it is bold. Sometimes it hides behind "collaboration."

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"I pulled together the strategy from your notes."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Let me walk them through the plan I developed."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="They say">
  <p>"Your draft helped, but I really shaped the final direction."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The theft is often wrapped in smooth language so objecting feels awkward. That is part of the design.

## Why it works on you

Credit theft hooks politeness and uncertainty. You may worry that correcting the record sounds petty, territorial, or difficult. Meanwhile, silence quietly becomes agreement.

The pattern is not shared ownership. Shared ownership names contributions. Credit theft blurs the source until the person with the microphone owns the room.

## The tell

Watch for your work becoming "input," "support," or "rough notes" while their role becomes "strategy," "lead," or "vision."

<ScriptBlock label="Anchor line">
  <p>"I want to clarify that I built the original strategy and draft."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Before it goes out">
  <p>"Please include my name on the work before it goes out."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Balanced">
  <p>"I am glad the idea is moving forward. I need my contribution represented accurately."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The tell is a shrinking description of your role.

## The trap

The trap is waiting until it feels big enough to object.

<ScriptBlock label="The line that feeds it">
  <p>"It is fine. They probably know I helped."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

Maybe they do. Maybe they do not. Clarity is cheaper early.

<ScriptBlock label="Better">
  <p>"Before we present this, let's list who owned which parts."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Exit line">
  <p>"If my contribution is not included, I am not comfortable sending this under your name alone."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

## Counters

I-Statement helps you correct without attacking. Boundary names the requirement. Consequence Setting is useful if the pattern repeats after direct correction.

<ScriptBlock label="I-statement">
  <p>"I am not comfortable with the draft being presented without my name attached."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Boundary">
  <p>"I need credit included before this is shared."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

<ScriptBlock label="Smallest usable version">
  <p>"Please add my name."</p>
</ScriptBlock>

The win is not sounding grand. The win is making authorship visible.
